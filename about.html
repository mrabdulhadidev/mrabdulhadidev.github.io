About

The full story — how I got here, what I've built, and where I'm headed.

I'm a self-taught developer based in Faisalabad, Pakistan. There was no single moment where I decided to become a programmer — it built up gradually, the way most self-taught skills do: one small script that worked, then a slightly bigger one, then a full app I could actually put in front of someone.

Somewhere in that process I started teaching what I was learning, almost by accident — explaining a concept to someone else forced me to actually understand it properly myself. That habit turned into Python for AI Engineers, a complete beginner-to-intermediate book written for people with no programming background at all. My mission with it is simple: make world-class technology education available to every student, in plain language, without gatekeeping. I believe that with the right explanation, the right pace, and the right practice, anyone can learn to code — and that students here deserve the same quality of technical education available anywhere else in the world.

Building real things

Writing about programming is one thing; shipping software people actually use is another. Elitra was the biggest test of that — a five-platform food delivery marketplace with four different user roles, built solo from the database up, load-tested to make sure it could actually hold up under real traffic instead of just looking good in a demo.

Elice pushed in a different direction: could a finance app run its AI assistant completely offline, with zero dependency on the internet or a cloud API? Getting a 1.5-billion-parameter model to run smoothly on-device took real trial and error, including redesigning the extraction pipeline in native Kotlin after the first approach hit a wall.

MCF came from something more personal — a working cattle farm that needed one clear dashboard instead of scattered paper records. And the Telegram bot was where a lot of this started: a small, complete project built end to end, on Termux, with nothing but a phone.

What I'm exploring now

Outside of client work, I'm interested in programming languages and compiler design — specifically, what better foundations for AI development might look like at the language level. I want to be upfront that this is a personal, long-term interest I'm still exploring, not a shipped project. I'd rather say that plainly than dress up an idea as something it isn't.
